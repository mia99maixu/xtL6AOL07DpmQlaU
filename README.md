# Term Deposit Marketing

### Background:

We are a small startup focusing mainly on providing machine learning solutions in the European banking market. We work on a variety of problems including fraud detection, sentiment classification and customer intention prediction and classification.

We are interested in developing a robust machine learning system that leverages information coming from call center data.

Ultimately, we are looking for ways to improve the success rate for calls made to customers for any product that our clients offer. Towards this goal we are working on designing an ever evolving machine learning product that offers high success outcomes while offering interpretability for our clients to make informed decisions.

### Goal(s):

Predict if the customer will subscribe (yes/no) to a term deposit (variable y)

Hit %81 or above accuracy by evaluating with 5-fold cross-validation and reporting the average performance score.

Find the customers who are more likely to buy the investment product. Determine the segment(s) of customers our client should prioritize. Find out What makes the customers buy? - Which feature should be the focuse be on.

### Data Description:

The data comes from the direct marketing efforts of a European banking institution. The marketing campaign involves making a phone call to a customer, often multiple times to ensure a product subscription, in this case, a term deposit. Term deposits are usually short-term deposits with maturities ranging from one month to a few years. When buying a term deposit, the customer must understand that they can withdraw their funds only after the term ends. All customer information that might reveal personal information is removed due to privacy concerns.

Attributes:

age : age of customer (numeric)

job : type of job (categorical)

marital : marital status (categorical)

education (categorical)

default: has credit in default? (binary)

balance: average yearly balance, in euros (numeric)

housing: has a housing loan? (binary)

loan: has a personal loan? (binary)

contact: contact communication type (categorical)

day: last contact day of the month (numeric)

month: last contact month of year (categorical)

duration: last contact duration, in seconds (numeric)

campaign: number of contacts performed during this campaign and for this client (numeric, includes last contact)

Output (desired target):

y - has the client subscribed to a term deposit? (binary)

### Methodology:

#### Part1: Exploratory Data Analysis(EDA):

Key visuals from the notebook (countplots, bar charts, and pair plots) illustrate these findings: the target imbalance, conversion rates by marital/loan/housing status, education and occupation heatmaps, and the long-call/high-balance characteristics of positive responders. Updated plots live in `Term_Deposit_Project1.2.ipynb` and can be regenerated by re-running the notebook.

2)Finding any relations or trends considering multiple features.

Pair plots and categorical overlays in the notebook highlight additional relationships—longer call duration, higher balances, and mid-life age bands correlate strongly with subscription. Refer to the notebook for the latest renders.


#### Part2: Feature Engineering and Data Cleaning:

1)Adding engineered features. Not necessary for this iteration because the raw attributes already provide strong lift once encoded.

2)Removing redundant features. Instead of mutating the original dataset with label encoders, categorical variables are one-hot encoded inside the modeling pipeline so we can retain the raw frame for analysis. The exploratory SelectKBest routine is now run on a temporary one-hot representation to inspect signal strength without feeding those engineered features directly into the model.

3)Converting features into a suitable form for modeling through a `ColumnTransformer` that standardizes the numeric columns (`age`, `balance`, `day`, `duration`, `campaign`) and one-hot encodes the categorical columns (`job`, `marital`, `education`, `default`, `housing`, `loan`, `contact`, `month`). The target `y` remains as the original yes/no labels externally and is mapped to 0/1 only for training.

#### Part3: Predictive Modeling

1)Running Basic Algorithms.

2)Cross Validation.

3)Ensembling.

4)Important Features Extraction.

5)Earlier baselines over-fit the majority class, but the class-weighted LightGBM pipeline lifts minority recall to 0.791 and raises the positive-class F1 to 0.571 while keeping overall accuracy above 91%. We retain a strong ROC-AUC profile (≈0.95) without needing synthetic oversampling, and the confusion matrix now shows materially fewer false negatives for subscribing customers.

#### Part4: Model Training Pipeline

1)Set the pipeline with a class-weighted LightGBM model. Rather than oversampling with SMOTE, the imbalance is addressed by using LightGBM's built-in `class_weight='balanced'` while the `ColumnTransformer` handles preprocessing:

```
preprocessor = ColumnTransformer([
    ('num', StandardScaler(), numeric_features),
    ('cat', OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown='ignore'), categorical_features),
])

pipeline = Pipeline([
    ('preprocessor', preprocessor),
    ('model', LGBMClassifier(
        random_state=42,
        n_estimators=400,
        learning_rate=0.05,
        num_leaves=63,
        min_child_samples=50,
        class_weight='balanced',
    )),
])
```

2)Evaluate the model. On the stratified hold-out set the pipeline reaches 91.4% accuracy, 0.571 F1 for the subscribing class, and 0.791 recall. A 5-fold cross-validation on the full dataset records an average accuracy of **91.4% ± 0.23%** and an average F1 of **0.568 ± 0.007**, comfortably beating the 81% accuracy target while materially improving minority-class recall.

3)Segment customers. Using the tuned pipeline, we score every customer and group them into Low (<0.30), Medium (0.30–0.60), and High (>0.60) propensity bands. The High segment (≈4.7k people) averages 62% observed conversion, the Medium band holds ~1.8k customers around 44% predicted probability, and the remaining ~33.5k customers fall in the Low segment with negligible conversion. This segmentation gives the marketing team an actionable call list.

4)Hyperparameter search is run on the same pipeline (tuning `n_estimators`, `learning_rate`, `num_leaves`, and `min_child_samples`) to confirm the above configuration is optimal for F1.

5)Model Explanation, use SHAP to explain the best model's predictions. With the updated preprocessing we compute SHAP values on the transformed design matrix so the plots stay in sync with the deployed pipeline.

SHAP summary, bar, dependence, and force plots in the notebook visualize the tuned model’s behaviour. They show duration and balance as the largest positive contributors, with heavier campaign counts and short calls pushing predictions toward the negative class. Month-of-contact, housing status, and marital status remain the key categorical levers.

### Conclusion:

Based on the refreshed SHAP analysis and permutation importance, call `duration` remains the single most influential driver, followed by `balance`, `age`, `day`, and `campaign` depth. Categorical lifts are most pronounced for `contact=cellular`, `housing=no`, and `marital=married`. These signals reinforce prioritizing well-funded, middle-aged clients who stay engaged longer on the call, and scheduling follow-ups when the monthly timing historically aligns with conversions.

The segmented scoring confirms this story: the High-propensity cohort (~4.7k customers) converts at ~62% and tends to be middle-aged, financially comfortable, and contacted via cellular channels. The Medium band (~1.8k customers, ~44% predicted probability) offers a promising secondary list, while everyone else falls into the Low band with negligible response. Marketing should therefore focus its limited call budget on the High group first, limit outreach to one or two well-timed calls, and keep messaging aligned with the traits uncovered above to maximise term-deposit uptake.
